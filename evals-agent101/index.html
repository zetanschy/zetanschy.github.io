Task

“Put the cap into the red cup.” The wording is taken verbatim from the π0.5 checkpoint’s fine-tuning instruction, because a VLA is only obliged to follow the instruction it was trained on — rewording it would hand the comparison to the LLM by default. Both policies get the same task object, the same embodiment and the same operator, and differ only in the policy.

The operator places the cap and cup by hand for each layout, then answers y/n after watching. Nothing on this rig can detect a cap entering a cup, so there is no automatic scorer.

Reading this honestly

  • Three trials against ten. Astra’s 1/3 and π0.5’s 8/10 are not equally well measured. At three trials the confidence interval is wide enough to include much better and much worse than 33%.
  • Astra’s one success collided with the cup. It counted because the cap ended up in the cup, which is what the instruction asked for.
  • The third Astra trial was assembled from an interrupted run. The operator cancelled the episode before the verdict prompt; the trial itself ran 38 decisions and its outcome was watched. The report records that substitution, including the original status, rather than presenting it as a clean run.
  • Cost is only defined for one side. π0.5 runs on a local GPU, so its trials carry no token bill — not that they are free. Comparing the two on cost means first deciding what local inference is worth.
  • Nothing here is a benchmark result. One arm, one bench, one operator, one afternoon.

Astra’s input tokens grow through a trial as camera frames accumulate in the conversation — 1.5k on the first call, 9.6k by the 38th — so a longer trial costs superlinearly. Cached-token reads were zero throughout, so none of it billed at the cached rate.
